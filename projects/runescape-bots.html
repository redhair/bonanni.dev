Origin story

RuneScape bots

Role
Sole author
Era
Teens, wound down 2017
Strategy
Cross-venue rune arbitrage
Peak
13 bots, ~100M gold/week

Most RuneScape bots mined gold for 24 hours and sold it. Mine found an edge instead. The rune shop sold runes at a price that rose as supply dropped. The Grand Exchange, the game’s global broker, ran the whole player economy. Impatient players paid a premium there for runes they could have walked to the shop to buy.

So the bot bought runes at 3 to 3.5 gold and sold them at 4. A cost tracker kept the average buy price below a threshold, which guaranteed the spread. It was an arbitrage on impatience, which I later learned is most arbitrages.

At peak I ran 13 bots on randomized sleep cycles, profiting around 100 million gold a week. I paid for the server space in Bitcoin, in 2017, from a forum user selling Canadian rack space. That was my first crypto transaction, and I did not think about it again for three years.

The lesson

The bots eventually got reported, and I let the project go. It had already paid out what mattered: a working mental model of market making, spreads, and inventory cost, learned inside a game, a decade before I built a real exchange.
